Project Overview

This project assessed COVID-19-related spatial risk and travel safety in Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, and Shenzhen. It combined entropy-weighted vulnerability and hazard scores, Random Forest analysis, and decision tree cellular automata (DT-CA) within a geospatial workflow.

The study addressed the need to examine variation within cities. A 1 km grid brought together epidemic observations and socioeconomic and built-environment indicators, supporting area-level assessment, map generation, and exploration of different control-policy scenarios.

02

Epidemic Risk Assessment

The risk assessment separated vulnerability from hazard. The indicator system combined recent case counts and their surrounding neighborhoods with regional GDP, GDP per capita, population density, hospital-bed availability, and the older population.

Indicators were normalized according to their direction of influence. The entropy weighting procedure used their distributions across samples to derive indicator weights and calculate vulnerability and hazard scores. These scores were combined into a risk assessment for each 1,000 m × 1,000 m grid cell.

Entropy weighting in six steps

Let \(x_{ij}\) denote the original value of indicator \(j\) for sample \(i\), with \(m\) samples and \(n\) indicators. The minimum and maximum for each indicator are taken across all samples.

  1. Normalize positively and negatively oriented indicators.

    For a positively oriented indicator:

    \[ z_{ij}=\frac{x_{ij}-\min(x_j)}{\max(x_j)-\min(x_j)} \]

    For a negatively oriented indicator:

    \[ z_{ij}=\frac{\max(x_j)-x_{ij}}{\max(x_j)-\min(x_j)} \]
  2. Calculate each sample's proportion within each indicator.
    \[ p_{ij}=\frac{z_{ij}}{\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{m}z_{rj}} \]
  3. Calculate the information entropy of each indicator.
    \[ e_j=-k\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{ij}\ln(p_{ij}) \]

    The normalization coefficient is \(k=1/\ln m\), with \(m>1\).

  4. Calculate information utility from the entropy values.
    \[ d_j=1-e_j \]
  5. Normalize information utility to obtain indicator weights.
    \[ w_j=\frac{d_j}{\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}d_{\ell}} \]
  6. Combine the weighted indicator contributions into sample scores.
    \[ s_i=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_jp_{ij} \]

Symbols. \(z_{ij}\) is the normalized value; \(p_{ij}\) is the sample proportion within indicator \(j\); \(e_j\), \(d_j\), and \(w_j\) are the indicator entropy, information utility, and weight; \(s_i\) is the composite score for sample \(i\).

Equations follow the main-text formulation of the translated report. The coefficient \(k=1/\ln m\) is specified using the entropy normalization shown in Figure 1. For \(p_{ij}=0\), use \(p_{ij}\ln(p_{ij})=0\). Nonzero indicator ranges and normalization denominators are required.

03

Travel Safety Assessment

The travel safety module used population density, OpenStreetMap road-network data, and points of interest to construct spatial predictor variables. Kernel density estimation, line density estimation, extraction of raster values to points, and spatial joins connected these predictors with the gridded distribution of infected people.

The Forest-based Classification and Regression tool in GeoScene Pro provided feature-importance scores. The workflow then applied those importance scores to grid-cell indicator values to calculate the travel safety score.

Random Forest classification workflow

Random Forest combines multiple decision trees. Each tree produces a classification result, and the ensemble assigns the class with the most votes. The project used the model's feature importance to support the spatial scoring workflow.

04

Epidemic Scenario Simulation

The scenario module used decision tree cellular automata to explore the spatial evolution of infected areas under different control policies. A cellular automaton represents space as a grid of cells, with discrete states and time steps. Local transition rules connect each cell's state with its neighborhood.

The report describes an implementation in ArcGIS 10.4 using GeoSOS for ArcGIS v2.0.0a. Spatial variables were prepared from points of interest, road networks, and a random raster. Historical and current seven-day cumulative trajectory data were processed to extract transition rules and train the decision-tree model.

Project Features & Resources

  • Grid-based analysis. The 1 km grid represents variation within each city and provides a common spatial unit for the assessment.
  • Multiple data sources. Epidemic observations, socioeconomic indicators, population, roads, and points of interest contribute to the risk and travel-safety workflows.
  • Automated GIS processing. ModelBuilder and ArcPy connect data processing, score calculation, and map production.
  • Policy scenario exploration. DT-CA links spatial transition rules with simulated epidemic patterns under different control policies.
