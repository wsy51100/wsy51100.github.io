Mobile Mapping System Evaluation

Comparing kinematic laser scanning with a georeferenced TLS reference.

Context
Master's course project
Institution
University of Bonn
Reference
Georeferenced static TLS

Team

Abu Talha, Sajjad Hussain, Shuyi Wu, Moein Taherkhani

This team project evaluates kinematic laser scanning against a georeferenced terrestrial laser scanning (TLS) reference. The study covers survey planning, static and kinematic acquisition, point-cloud processing, and geometric comparison at a building survey site in Bonn.

01

Survey & Acquisition

The scanning plan places TLS stations, GNSS receivers, artificial targets, and a kinematic measurement trajectory around the survey site. Static observations provide the reference for the subsequent mobile mapping comparison.

Static measurements

A terrestrial laser scanner, GNSS observations, and artificial targets support scan registration and georeferencing. The project checks registration and georeferencing consistency before comparing the point clouds.

Kinematic measurements

The kinematic survey uses the Z+F Profiler 9020c. The registration workflow combines profile-scanner observations with system calibration, sensor synchronization, and trajectory transformations into global coordinates.

Acquisition and georeferencing: presentation slides 4–21.

02

Processing Workflow

The point-cloud workflow applies subsampling and statistical outlier filtering, followed by ground removal and connected-component filtering. ICP then aligns the kinematic cloud to the fixed TLS reference before evaluation.

View the sensor-to-world transformation model in slide 19.

03

Evaluation Methods

The study compares the processed point clouds through five approaches. These examine point distances, surface offsets, local plane geometry, and feature correspondences.

Method Comparison
C2C Unsigned nearest-point distances from the kinematic cloud to the TLS reference.
C2M Signed distances to a mesh reconstructed from the TLS reference.
M3C2 Signed cloud comparison along locally estimated normal directions.
Plane fitting Plane-fitting RMS and tilt for a selected building façade.
Keypoints ISS keypoint selection and correspondence validation using FPFH descriptors.

Evaluation framework: presentation slide 27.

Local Geometry & Keypoints

Selected façade plane

Reported façade comparison from slide 33.

Keypoint correspondences

The point-based evaluation uses ISS keypoints and FPFH descriptors. From 1,500 selected keypoints, it reports 191 mutual correspondences and 103 validated matches, or 54% of the mutual correspondences.

Keypoint workflow and matching counts: slides 34–35.

06

Findings & Resources

  • The kinematic point cloud reproduces the overall building geometry in the survey area.
  • C2M and M3C2 report positive mean signed offsets in their comparisons with the TLS reference.
  • The selected façade shows lower plane-fitting RMS for TLS, supporting its role as the geometric reference in this study.
