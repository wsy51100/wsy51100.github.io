Monitoring Pine Wilt Disease-Infected Trees Using UAV Remote Sensing and Deep Learning Algorithms

This team project investigates how UAV imagery can support the identification and mapping of visibly infected and dead pine trees in the mountainous region of southern Jiangxi. It compares pixel segmentation with object detection and combines their outputs with forest-area extraction.

The workflow connects aerial image mosaicking, visual sample annotation, spectral and texture features, deep learning, and geospatial postprocessing.

Background

Pine wilt disease threatens pine forests through its rapid spread, high mortality among infected trees, and difficulty of control. The problem is particularly relevant to southern Jiangxi, where extensive and contiguous stands of Masson pine provide conditions for the disease to spread. Efficient monitoring is therefore important for identifying affected trees and supporting forest management.

Conventional monitoring relies on manual ground surveys, which are time-consuming and labor-intensive. UAV remote sensing provides aerial imagery that can be combined with deep learning to support rapid, automated identification of visibly affected pine crowns. Changes in needle color and crown structure, from yellowing and reddish-brown foliage to exposed pale branches after needle loss, provide visual cues for distinguishing symptomatic and dead infected trees.

02

Study Area & Image Acquisition

The study focuses on Ganzhou in southern Jiangxi, including training and validation areas in Huichang and Yudu counties. Hilly terrain and extensive Masson pine stands motivate the use of aerial imagery for forest monitoring.

The acquisition setup uses a DJI Phantom 4 at a flight altitude of 150 m. Flight planning considers tree distribution, elevation, UAV range, and forward and side overlap. Pix4Dmapper produces orthomosaics, which are cropped and prepared in ENVI.

Visual interpretation distinguishes healthy, early-infected, middle-stage, and late-stage crowns. The project targets visible symptoms and dead infected trees in RGB imagery.

03

Technical Workflow

UAV images are mosaicked, aligned to a common coordinate system, and cropped to remove blurred edges. The workflow then builds sample libraries, prepares spectral and texture features, trains and compares two deep learning routes, and intersects predictions with an extracted forest mask. Visual accuracy assessment guides ROI refinement and retraining.

04

Sample Annotation

The presentation reports a library of 1,143 infected-tree and dead infected-tree samples. ENVI ROIs delineate tree boundaries. Sampling is refined from one training area to multiple areas with dense samples and varied land cover.

Target Visual interpretation Annotation guidance
Infected trees Yellowish brown, reddish brown or brown crowns with compact texture. Trace the crown boundary consistently.
Dead infected trees Gray, open crowns with clearly visible branches. Delineate only the central portion and keep boundaries small.

05

Spectral & Texture Features

Three visible-light bands are combined with eight texture statistics: mean, variance, homogeneity, contrast, dissimilarity, entropy, angular second moment, and correlation. The segmentation setup uses the resulting 11-band input.

Principal component analysis supplies the first component for texture extraction in ENVI. Co-occurrence Measures generates the texture bands, which are stacked and normalized to a common range.

Slides 20-22Feature Preparation
06

Pixel Segmentation

The segmentation route uses ENVI Deep Learning's TensorFlow-based ENVINet5, an encoder-decoder architecture based on U-Net. It assigns labels to pixels and represents infected-tree regions as irregular patches.

Initialization uses patch size 464, 11 bands, and one class. The described procedure creates label rasters for three training areas and builds two initial models. Randomized parameter-group training produces 25 candidates, BEST00-BEST24. BEST12, BEST13, and BEST15 are shortlisted; comparison against visually interpreted samples in the validation area leads to the selection of BEST12.

Slides 23-27Deep Learning & Pixel Segmentation
07

Object Detection

Sample ROIs are exported as shapefiles, converted to rectangles and imported as rectangular ROIs to create object-detection label rasters. The training procedure specifies 25 epochs. Classification uses a confidence threshold of 0.8 and an IoU setting of 0.1.

The source procedure supplies the same label rasters for training and validation. Its model-initialization, labeling, training and inference steps are retained in slides 28-31.

Slides 28-31Object Detection
08

Forest-mask Postprocessing

Bare ground and buildings are major sources of false detections. Forest-area extraction supplies a spatial mask for excluding predictions on non-forest land cover, including dry grass outside forest stands.

Multiresolution segmentation groups pixels by color and shape similarity. Comparison of scales including 500, 600 and 700 leads to a final scale parameter of approximately 600. The extracted forest area is intersected with model outputs, followed by cluster-based removal.

09

Results & Interpretation

The reported method comparison uses visually interpreted trees as the reference. Pixel segmentation gives higher producer's accuracy and lower omission error; object detection gives higher user's accuracy but misses a larger proportion of reference trees.

Method User's accuracy Producer's accuracy Commission error Omission error
Pixel segmentation 0.44 0.97 0.56 0.03
Object detection 0.70 0.55 0.29 0.45

Values reproduced as reported in slide 37. User's accuracy is correct detections divided by all detections; producer's accuracy is correct detections divided by the visually interpreted reference count.

Project Outcome

The project identifies pixel segmentation as the more practical route for infected-tree extraction in this workflow. Combining UAV imagery, labeled samples, texture features and forest-mask postprocessing provides a way to locate visibly affected trees and support forestry field investigation.
